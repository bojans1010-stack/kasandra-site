What moves the price of gold

Before any trade, understand the forces you are up against.

The US dollar & interest rates

Gold is priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar usually pushes gold down and a weaker dollar lifts it. Rising interest rates make cash more attractive than gold; falling rates do the opposite.

Fear & safe-haven demand

When markets panic (war, banking stress, recession fear), money often flows into gold as a store of value. This is why gold can spike hard on news.

Sessions & volatility

Gold is most active during the London and New York sessions. More movement means more opportunity, and more risk. Quiet hours often chop sideways.

Risk & position sizing

This is the part that decides whether you survive. Most beginners skip it.

The 1–2% rule

Never risk more than 1–2% of your account on a single trade. On a $500 account that is $5–$10 of risk per trade. Boring, yes. It is also why disciplined traders are still around next year.

Know your $ per point

On gold, 0.01 lot means roughly $1 for every $1 the price moves; 1.00 lot means roughly $100. Same trade, 100× the swing, up and down. Always know your $/point before you click buy.

Always use a stop loss

Decide your maximum loss in dollars before you enter, and set a hard stop there. A fixed, pre-planned loss is a cost of doing business. An unplanned one is how accounts disappear.

The 5 discipline rules

Print these. They matter more than any entry.

  • Fixed risk every trade. Same small percentage, no exceptions, no "this one is different".
  • One entry, one plan. No adding to losers, no martingale, no doubling down to "get it back".
  • Hard stop, always. Know your exit before your entry. Let it do its job.
  • No revenge trading. After a loss, step away. Chasing is how a bad hour becomes a bad month.
  • Journal every trade. Win or loss. You cannot improve what you do not measure.
